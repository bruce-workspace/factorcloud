Rate compression in factoring is real. As the industry matures, the spread between the best and worst rates a client can get has narrowed. Competing on price alone is a race to the bottom that no one wins.

The operators building durable businesses in this environment aren't doing it on rate. They're doing it on integration — on the depth of connection between their software stack and their clients' operations.

## What "Connected" Actually Means

A connected factoring stack isn't just a platform with an API. It's a system where:

- Your accounting sync (QuickBooks, Sage) runs automatically, so your clients' books stay current without anyone touching a CSV.
- Your credit verification (Ansonia, ROX) fires on every new debtor, automatically, before a funding decision is made.
- Your payment processing (Tank Payments) is embedded in the workflow, so clients submit invoices and receive funds in the same system.
- Your document processing (BrightBolt OCR) handles invoice extraction before a human ever sees the document.
- Your mobile access (FactorGenie) means clients can submit and track from anywhere.

When all of this works together, something interesting happens: your clients stop thinking of you as a lender and start thinking of you as infrastructure. And infrastructure is very hard to leave.

## The Switching Cost Equation

Here's the dirty secret of client retention in factoring: most clients don't leave because of rate. They leave because the relationship sours or because a competitor makes switching feel easy.

A connected stack changes the switching cost calculation dramatically. If your client's accounting is synced to your platform, their debtor history is in your system, their team is trained on your portal, and their payment flows run through your infrastructure — switching isn't a weekend project. It's a multi-month operational migration.

That's not lock-in through friction (which breeds resentment). It's lock-in through value — the integrations are saving them time and money, so leaving means giving that up.

## The 20+ Integration Benchmark

We've found that 20+ active integrations represents a meaningful inflection point for factoring platforms. Below that number, the integrations feel like features. Above it, they feel like a network — and networks have compounding value.

FactorCloud's integration roster currently includes 20+ partners across:
- **Accounting**: QuickBooks Online
- **Credit & Risk**: Ansonia, ROX, Decipher
- **Payments**: Tank Payments, Bill360, Triumph Business Capital
- **Document Processing**: BrightBolt OCR
- **Mobile**: FactorGenie
- **Freight & Transport**: CargoNerd, Trucker Cloud
- **Banking**: BankShot
- **AI**: Claude, Lighthouz AI, Peruse, Factorgenie

Each integration adds marginal value to every other integration in the stack, because data flows more freely and workflows close more completely.

## What to Build First

If you're evaluating how to build a connected stack, the sequencing matters. Start with:

1. **Accounting sync** — This is the integration your clients will notice first and appreciate most. Eliminating the QuickBooks export/import dance alone is often enough to justify the switch.

2. **Automated credit verification** — This protects your portfolio and speeds up debtor approvals. It should feel invisible once it's working.

3. **Client portal with structured submission** — Once clients submit through the portal, OCR accuracy improves, email volumes drop, and your intake team can handle significantly more volume.

The remaining integrations layer on top of this foundation. Each one adds value, but the first three create the conditions for the others to be useful.

## The Competitive Implication

Here's where this gets strategic. If your stack is connected and your competitor's isn't, you can process more volume per head, make faster funding decisions, and offer a better client experience — all simultaneously.

That's not a feature advantage. That's a structural cost advantage that compounds over time, because you can grow without proportionally growing headcount.

The factors winning in 2025 figured this out early. The ones catching up are doing it quickly, because the tools are accessible. The question is execution — which is always the question.
